# AI Usage Disclosure

This document discloses how AI tools were used in the development of **Cratera**, in line with the transparency requirements of the NASA Space Apps Challenge.

## 1. Summary

**No generative AI is used at runtime.** All solar/Earth ephemeris calculations, horizon occlusion checks, visibility timings, 3D terrain relief, and mission statistics in Cratera are computed deterministically by code from mathematical orbital models and NASA LRO/LOLA datasets.

AI tools were used only during development, under human direction and review:

| Tool | Role | Used for |
|------|------|----------|
| **ChatGPT** | Brainstorming and support | Brainstorming, prompt writing, debugging issues |
| **Claude** | Brainstorming and support | Brainstorming, prompt writing, debugging issues |
| **Google AI Studio** | Build assistant (web application) | React/TypeScript UI scaffolding, Three.js 3D scene and GLSL shader implementation, deterministic data-generation/validation scripts, unit test authoring, debugging |
| **Google Antigravity** (Google DeepMind agentic coding assistant) | Build assistant (geospatial data pipeline) | Horizon ray-tracing engine, coordinate transformations, DEM inspection, high-resolution validation, plot/data generation scripts |

## 3. Google AI Studio (Web Application Build)

Google AI Studio was used strictly as an AI coding and prototyping assistant during development.

### A. Project Scaffolding and Build Setup

- Generated the initial React 19 + TypeScript + Vite + Tailwind CSS v4 application template (`package.json`, `vite.config.ts`, `tsconfig.json`, `index.html`, `metadata.json`, `.env.example`).
- **Note for disclosure accuracy:** Although the starter `package.json` includes `@google/genai` and `.env.example` lists `GEMINI_API_KEY` from the default AI Studio template, no AI SDK or Gemini API is imported or called anywhere in `src/` or `scripts/`.

### B. Application Architecture and UI Implementation

AI Studio assisted in writing and iterating on the frontend components and pages:

- **Navigation and pages:** `Layout.tsx`, `PlannerPage.tsx`, `ComparePage.tsx`, `AboutPage.tsx`
- **2D analysis and controls:**
  - `SkyView.tsx` (360° SVG horizon silhouette, Sun/Earth elevation markers, zoom/centering)
  - `TimeScrubber.tsx` (UTC date/time pickers, playback controls, +14d slip, and Next Sunrise / Next Sunset / Next Earthrise / Next Earthset jump shortcuts)
  - `NumberCards.tsx` (Sun elevation, Time until sunset, Earth visible yes/no, Time until Earthset, Sunlit % over a 29.53-day lunar day, and Longest dark period)
  - `TimelineStrips.tsx` and `IllumHeatmap.tsx` (29.53-day visibility strips and 90-day × 24-hour UTC illumination heatmap)
  - `CompareGrid.tsx`, `WhatChanged.tsx`, `SitePicker.tsx`, `FloatingSitesWindow.tsx`, and `TechnicalPanel.tsx`

### C. 3D WebGL Visualization (Three.js and GLSL)

AI Studio assisted in building and debugging the interactive Three.js viewports:

- `MoonGlobe3D.tsx`: 3D Moon globe with orbit presets, projected site pins, and custom GLSL shader logic blending the high-resolution South Pole polar stereographic cap onto the global Moon sphere.
- `LunarScene3D.tsx` and `src/components/scene3d/` (`terrain.ts`, `lander.ts`, `deepSpace.ts`, `textures.ts`): local terrain relief, lander model geometry, procedural regolith micro-detail/normal maps, and deep-space starfield rendering.

### D. Deterministic Math, Data Pipelines and Testing

- Assisted in authoring the non-AI, deterministic calculation modules in `src/lib/` (`geometry.ts`, `events.ts`, `windows.ts`, `heatmap.ts`, `data.ts`, `nasaTerrainProfiles.ts`).
- Assisted in writing:
  - `scripts/generate-lunar-data.mjs` (Meeus orbital elements + Cassini libration model and site horizon profiles)
  - `scripts/generate-summaries.mjs`
  - `scripts/check-contract.mjs`
  - Vitest unit tests: `src/lib/__tests__/events.test.ts` and `src/lib/__tests__/windows.test.ts`

### E. Debugging and Iterative Refinement

Used interactively to diagnose and resolve technical issues during development, for example:

- Fixing the GLSL shader compilation/blending issue in `MoonGlobe3D.tsx`
- Adjusting responsive HUD overlays
- Surfacing the lunar-day statistics and date/time + sunrise/sunset shortcuts
- Diagnosing the Windows `npm install` peer-dependency conflict

### F. What Was Not Generated by AI (Web Application)

- **Runtime calculations:** Zero LLM/AI inference happens in the browser or on a server. Every number shown in the UI (`sunlitPct`, `longestDarkHours`, `margin`, `timeUntil`, and +14d deltas) is computed deterministically from `public/data/*.json`.
- **NASA scientific textures and reference data (`public/textures/`):**
  - Global lunar albedo, elevation, and normal maps from NASA SVS #4720 / LROC WAC and LRO LOLA (`lroc_color_4k.*`, `lola_elev_4k.webp`, `lola_normal_4k.webp`).
  - South Pole polar stereographic relief and normal maps from NASA PDS LRO LOLA (`LDEM_80S_80M.IMG`) (`lola_sp_80s_relief_2k.webp`, `lola_sp_80s_normal_2k.webp`, `sp_site_patches.json`).
  - Deep-space starfield from NASA SVS #4851 Deep Star Maps 2020 (`starmap_2020_4k.webp`, `starmap_4k.jpg`).
- **Human direction and verification:** Site selection, mission-planning requirements, UI/UX decisions, and final review/testing were directed by the team.

The in-app **About and Method** page (`/about`, Section 7) also includes a concise AI Tool Use Disclosure stating that no generative AI runs at runtime and that AI coding assistance was used during scaffolding, UI layout, and unit test authoring under human review.

## 4. Google Antigravity (Lunar Geospatial Data Pipeline)

**AI tool used:** Google Antigravity (pair programming / agentic coding assistant).

### Scope of AI Assistance

Antigravity was used to pair-program and implement the lunar geospatial data pipeline. Specifically, it assisted in writing:

- Coordinate transformations with `pyproj` using the Moon's reference sphere (R = 1,737,400 m) to map lunar latitude/longitude to South Polar Stereographic coordinates.
- The mathematical derivation to convert compass azimuth to local True North in a polar stereographic projection.
- The vectorized ray-tracing algorithm in Python (`rasterio`, `numpy`, `scipy`) that computes 360-degree horizon profiles out to 60 km, correcting for lunar spherical curvature and observer mast height.
- Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) windowed reads to evaluate 80 m vs. 40 m LOLA elevation datasets.

### Detailed Breakdown

#### 4.1 Environment and Geospatial Setup

- Virtual environment dependency setup and configuration of geospatial C-extension libraries (`rasterio`, `pyproj`, `scipy`, `matplotlib`) on Windows.
- Created `.gitignore` to prevent committing raw DEM files (~180 MB to 700 MB) to Git.
- File: `.gitignore`

#### 4.2 DEM Inspection and Coordinate Translation

- Developed `inspect_and_locate.py` to read metadata directly from the NASA LOLA GeoTIFF (`LDEM_80S_80MPP_ADJ.TIF`), detect the coordinate reference system (IAU:30135), and project each landing site's coordinates from geographic degrees to grid meters and pixel row/column coordinates.
- Functions: `run_step_1()`, `run_step_2()`.

#### 4.3 Mathematical Modeling and Horizon Ray-Tracing Engine

Developed `compute_horizon.py`, containing:

- **Local True North and East derivation:** Calculated True North unit vectors (û_north) and conformal True East vectors (û_east) per site to translate compass azimuths into 2D grid directions (`get_local_north_and_east()`).
- **Vectorized horizon ray tracing:** Vectorized ray sampling across 360 azimuth rays (each stepping 80 m outward to 60 km, 270,000 points total) using bilinear sub-pixel interpolation (`scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates`).
- **Lunar curvature drop:** Implemented the exact spherical drop equation, `drop = R − √(R² − d²)` (R = 1,737,400 m), and mast height parameterization (`compute_horizon_site()`).

#### 4.4 High-Resolution Upgrade and Comparative Validation

- Added the `--res 40` pipeline to stream windowed reads from NASA's tiled Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF (`LDEM_80S_40MPP_ADJ.TIF`), allowing comparison between 80 m and 40 m terrain horizons without consuming excessive memory.
- **Verification results:**
  - Mean absolute difference: 0.18°
  - RMS difference: 0.37°
  - Maximum localized obstacle difference: +1.14°

#### 4.5 Data Deliverables and Visual Artifacts

Generated by scripts written with AI assistance:

- Standard JSON profiles in `output/horizon/` for all 6 South Pole sites and 2 Apollo equatorial contrast sites, matching the team's `CONTRACT.md`.
- Linear horizon curves, polar compass radar plots, and terrain hillshades with True North arrows in `output/plots/`.
- Compressed South Pole regional hillshade map background (`output/south_pole_hillshade.png`, 1.61 MB).

## 5. Human Oversight and Contributions

All scientific requirements, physical formulas, site selections, parameter limits, and data contract specifications were established by human team members.

- **Architecture and scope:** Decided on the multi-role division of labor (P1 through P5) and authored the `CONTRACT.md` data schema.
- **Scientific formulation:** Directed the use of LOLA polar stereographic datasets, the chosen observer radius (60 km), 1-degree azimuth bins, and mast heights (0 m / 2 m).
- **Site curation:** Selected the 6 Artemis candidate landing sites and the Apollo contrast sites from official NASA mission releases.
- **Ground-truth validation:** Compared output elevations and horizon peaks against NASA QuickMap and LOLA topographic charts.
- **Web application direction:** Site selection, mission-planning requirements, UI/UX decisions, and final review/testing were directed by the team.
- **Git and release management:** All Git operations (commits, branches, pull requests, merges) and release deployments were performed manually by team members.
